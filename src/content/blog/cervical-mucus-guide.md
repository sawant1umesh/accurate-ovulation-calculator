Monitoring changes in your cervical mucus (cervical fluid) is one of the most reliable, cost-effective methods to identify your fertile window in real-time. This fluid is regulated by fluctuating estrogen and progesterone levels.

## The Four Stages of Cervical Mucus

Throughout your cycle, pay attention to the dampness and texture of your discharge:

1. **Dry / Scant (Post-Period):** Immediately following menstruation, estrogen is low. You will notice little to no discharge, and your vulva will feel dry. Fertility is very low.
2. **Sticky / Creamy (Follicular Phase):** As estrogen begins to rise, the fluid turns thick, pasty, white, or cloudy. It feels sticky to the touch. This fluid acts as a barrier, limiting sperm motility. Fertility remains low.
3. **Wet / Watery (Approaching Ovulation):** As estrogen levels climb higher, the fluid becomes thin, watery, clear, and highly lubricative. It will make your underwear feel damp. This type of fluid allows sperm to swim easily. Fertility is high.
4. **Egg-White Cervical Mucus (EWCM - Peak Fertility):** Directly preceding ovulation, estrogen peaks. The mucus becomes highly clear, slippery, and stretchy—you can stretch it several inches between your fingers without it breaking. It resembles raw egg whites. This fluid nurtures sperm and aids their journey through the uterus. Fertility is at its peak.

## How to Check Your Cervical Mucus

To check consistency:
- Wash your hands thoroughly.
- Gently insert a clean finger into your vagina to obtain a sample near your cervix, or wipe the opening of your vagina with clean toilet tissue.
- Observe the color, texture, and stretchiness between your index finger and thumb. Log these observations daily.
