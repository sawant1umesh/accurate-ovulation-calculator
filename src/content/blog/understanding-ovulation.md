Ovulation is the central event of the female reproductive cycle. Understanding the biological mechanisms behind egg release can help you determine your most fertile days and optimize your chances of conception.

## The Menstrual Cycle Phases

A typical menstrual cycle lasts between 21 and 35 days, divided into four distinct stages:

1. **The Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5):** The cycle starts on the first day of your period bleeding. Progesterone and estrogen levels drop, signaling the uterus to shed its lining.
2. **The Follicular Phase (Days 6–13):** Guided by Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), the ovaries develop several fluid-filled sacs called follicles. One dominant follicle eventually grows larger than the rest, producing rising amounts of estrogen. Estrogen stimulates the rebuilding of the uterine lining (endometrium).
3. **The Ovulatory Phase (Day 14):** Rising estrogen triggers a sharp surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge causes the dominant follicle to rupture, releasing the mature egg into the fallopian tube.
4. **The Luteal Phase (Days 15–28):** The empty follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone stabilizes the endometrial lining to prepare for potential embryo implantation. If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels decline, leading to menstruation and the start of a new cycle.

## The Role of the LH Surge

The Luteinizing Hormone (LH) surge is the biological green light for ovulation. LH levels surge approximately 24 to 36 hours before the egg is officially released. By monitoring this surge using urine-based Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs), you can predict the exact day of ovulation and time intercourse for maximum probability of fertilization.
