After ovulation, the wait to find out if you have conceived can be filled with anticipation. Recognizing early signs of pregnancy can help you understand what is happening in your body as hormone levels begin to rise.

## The Implantation Window (6–10 DPO)

If fertilization was successful, the developing embryo travels down the fallopian tube and implants itself in the uterine lining. Implantation typically occurs between **6 and 10 days post ovulation (DPO)**.

Some women experience **implantation spotting**—a very light, pinkish or brownish discharge that lasts for a few hours to a couple of days, often accompanied by mild uterine twinges.

## Earliest Physical Symptoms

As soon as implantation succeeds, the placenta begins producing Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG). This rise in hCG, combined with surging progesterone, triggers early symptoms:

- **Breast Sensitivity:** Breasts may feel heavy, tender, or tingle. The areolas may darken.
- **Fatigue:** Surging progesterone levels can make you feel unusually exhausted.
- **Mild Cramping:** Light, centralized pelvic cramping as the uterus expands and accommodates the embryo.
- **Nausea or Sensitivity to Smells:** Often starting around week 6, but mild aversion or metallic taste in the mouth can appear earlier.

## When to Take a Test

While you may feel early symptoms, the most reliable way to confirm pregnancy is a home pregnancy test. Testing on the **first day of your missed period** (roughly 14 DPO) yields 99% accuracy. Testing earlier can result in a false negative because hCG levels are not yet high enough in the urine.
